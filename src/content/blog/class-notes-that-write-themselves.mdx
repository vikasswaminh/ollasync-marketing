The person who teaches a class is the worst-placed person to take its notes. Teaching *is* the attention; the recap always loses. So most live courses end with either no notes, a student's partial notes, or a trainer spending their evening reconstructing what they said that morning.

Here's how the [AI classroom](/) removes that job — and the one place it deliberately refuses to.

## From audio to a record, automatically

When a class is recorded, three artifacts are produced without anyone doing anything:

**The transcript.** The same streaming recognition that powers [live translation](/blog/how-live-voice-translation-works-in-class/) produces a full text of the session, attributed to speakers. It's searchable — which turns "I know we covered this in week 3" from a memory exercise into a lookup.

**The summary.** A structured recap of what was actually taught: the topics in order, the key explanations, what was assigned. It reads like the notes of a diligent student who never got distracted.

**The action items.** The "for next time" layer — exercises set, documents promised, questions parked — pulled out separately so they don't drown in prose.

All three attach to the session and its replay in your workspace. A ten-week course accumulates ten of each, [access-controlled](/features/recordings) to the cohort, forming the course archive as a side effect of simply teaching it.

## Ask the course a question

The assistant can answer questions against your recorded sessions: *"What did we tell the March cohort about the certification retake policy?"* — answered from the transcript, not from anyone's memory. For trainers running many cohorts, this replaces the "search your own inbox and hope" workflow entirely.

## The deliberate exception: E2EE classes are AI-free

Some sessions run [end-to-end encrypted](/security) — video, chat and screen share readable only by the participants. In those sessions, **no AI processing happens, by design**. That's not a limitation we apologise for; it's the definition holding: if a server-side system could transcribe your class, your class wouldn't be end-to-end encrypted. You choose per session — a public cohort class with full notes, or a sensitive internal session where nothing in the middle can read anything.

## What this replaces

- The unpaid evening job of writing recaps.
- The dedicated note-taker in corporate training sessions.
- The "can you send the notes?" thread after every class.
- The compliance guesswork of *what was this cohort actually told?* — the summary is the record.
