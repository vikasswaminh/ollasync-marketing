# Chapter 1: The Direct Answer & Executive Summary

## The Direct Answer (BLUF): Why GoToWebinar Costs More & What to Use Instead

GoToWebinar is expensive because it operates on a legacy software delivery model built in the early 2000s. Its high pricing structure—ranging from $49 to over $399 per organizer/month (billed annually) with sharp jumps at scale—stems from three structural factors:

1. **Legacy Infrastructure Overhead:** GoToWebinar relies on heavy desktop client architectures rather than lightweight, modern WebRTC protocols, requiring costly ongoing maintenance of native applications across operating systems.
2. **Attendee-Gated Monetization:** The platform enforces strict participant tiers (e.g., 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 3,000 attendees). Exceeding these thresholds locks out attendees or triggers punitive overage pricing and forced tier upgrades.
3. **Feature Fragmentation & Add-On Monetization:** Core capabilities that are standard in modern platforms—such as automated funnel workflows, multi-track hosting, continuous engagement engines, and deep bidirectional CRM syncs—are either gated behind higher enterprise tiers or missing entirely.

For marketing, sales, product demo, and corporate training teams, modern **gotowebinar alternatives** deliver superior engagement rates, browser-native accessibility (zero downloads), and lower total cost of ownership (TCO). 

### Top GoToWebinar Alternatives by Use Case

### 1. The Cost Breakdown: Why GoToWebinar Remains Exceptionally Expensive

To understand the price disparity, we must examine the cost centers baked into GoToWebinar’s pricing topology compared to modern platforms.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  LEGACY COST ENGINE (GoToWebinar)                       |

                                    vs.

|                                    ▼                                    |
|                      LEAN, USAGE-BASED EFFICIENCY                       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### A. Centralized Multipoint Control Units (MCU) vs. Selective Forwarding Units (SFU)
GoToWebinar’s foundational streaming stack historically relied on heavy MCU processing. In an MCU environment, the central server receives video and audio streams from every presenter, decodes them, composites them into a single video grid, re-encodes the stream, and broadcasts it to attendees. 

* **The Operational Impact:** Heavy video transcode operations run continuously on dedicated compute instances, creating immense compute costs per streaming hour.
* **The Modern Alternative:** Contemporary platforms utilize Selective Forwarding Units (SFU) or hybrid WebRTC-to-HLS/CMAF (Common Media Application Format) pipelines. SFUs do not decode or re-encode video at the media server level; they inspect and route individual media streams directly to endpoints. This reduces server-side CPU overhead by up to 80%, directly lowering the base operational cost.

#### B. Legacy PSTN/Toll-Free Audio Routing Infrastructure
GoToWebinar still maintains deep integration with legacy telecommunications carriers to provide dial-in phone numbers across dozens of countries. 

* **The Operational Impact:** Maintaining global carrier interconnect agreements, physical SIP gateways, and fallback toll-free routing costs millions annually. GoToWebinar amortizes this fixed telecom footprint across its user base, billing all subscribers regardless of whether their audiences use browser audio (VoIP) or cellular dial-in.
* **The Modern Alternative:** Browser-native alternatives prioritize 100% WebRTC Opus audio with adaptive jitter buffers and acoustic echo cancellation (AEC). By eliminating carrier-grade PSTN trunks, modern vendors eliminate one of the largest hidden line items in legacy web conferencing balance sheets.

#### C. Arbitrary Capacity Paywalls and Inflexible Tiering
GoToWebinar enforces rigid attendee caps (e.g., 250, 500, 1,000, or 3,000 attendees). If an enterprise anticipates 550 attendees, it must upgrade to the 1,000-seat tier, paying a 100% premium for 45% unallocated capacity.

* **The Problem:** You pay for *potential* concurrent capacity, not *realized* resource consumption.
* **The Modern Alternative:** Leading modern platforms feature dynamic concurrency scaling, event-based licensing, or host-based billing where attendee scaling is detached from punitive tier cliffs.
