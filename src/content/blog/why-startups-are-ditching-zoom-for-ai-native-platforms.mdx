### The "Bolted-On" AI Illusion: Why Zoom’s Translation Breaks

To support international teams, global customer roundtables, or multilingual webinars, legacy platforms rely on an increasingly fragile stack of bolt-on features:

1. **Headless Bot Insertion:** When you add live transcription or third-party translation to Zoom, the platform injects a headless participant (a virtual bot) into the room. This bot captures raw audio streams, sends them to an offsite server, processes the text, and routes it back.
2. **Cascading Pipeline Latency:** The traditional translation chain follows a slow, sequential path:
   
```text
Audio In \longrightarrow ASR (Transcription) \longrightarrow Machine Translation \longrightarrow TTS (Voice Synth) \longrightarrow Viewer Out
```

   Each handoff between isolated services incurs serialization, network overhead, and processing delay. Total latency frequently tops **3,000 to 5,000 milliseconds**. 
3. **Contextual Drift:** Because the transcription and translation layers run independently without room context, domain-specific startup vernacular—terms like "runway," "burn rate," "Kubernetes cluster," or "LTV:CAC"—breaks down into gibberish across languages.

For an international webinar or live pitch, a 4-second delay ruins interaction. Q&A sessions turn awkward, conversational flow stalls, and cross-border audiences are relegated to passive, second-class observers.
