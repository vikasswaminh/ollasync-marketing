### 1. The Registration vs. Attendee Cliff

The most common trap in the webinar market is the deliberate conflation of **Registrations** and **Concurrent Attendees**.

Virtual events rarely see 100% turn-out. Across B2B SaaS, healthcare, and financial services, average show-up rates hover reliably between 30% and 45%. 

Legacy pricing models exploit this variance using two distinct methods:

*   **The Hard Attendee Lock:** You buy a "500-attendee plan." You drive 1,500 registrations. If 550 people show up on the day of the event, attendee number 501 receives an error screen: *"The room is at capacity."* You have spent money on paid media, outbound email, and sales development to generate pipeline, only to have your vendor lock the door on 50 interested buyers.
*   **The Punitive Overage Model:** Other vendors let attendee 501 enter, but trigger automatic overage buckets. Instead of billing you for the incremental 50 attendees, they automatically upgrade your account to the 1,000- or 2,000-attendee tier for the remainder of the billing cycle—or charge punitive spot-rates that can reach $2.50 per attendee per session.

Modern cloud distribution does not justify this pricing jump. The raw egress cost of serving high-definition video through a global Content Delivery Network (CDN) scales linearly at fractions of a cent per gigabyte. 

When a platform charges you $300 more to move from 500 to 1,000 attendees for a 60-minute call, you aren't paying for infrastructure. You are paying a success tax.

### The Two Video Topologies Driving Base Costs

Every webinar platform runs on one of two primary WebRTC video distribution models. Your subscription invoice is dictated by which one the vendor chose a decade ago:

#### 1. Multipoint Control Units (MCU)
MCUs take all incoming video and audio streams, decode them, stitch them into a single video composite on a central server, re-encode it, and push it to every viewer. 

*   **The Technical Reality:** Extreme central CPU usage. Every additional presenter or attendee adds exponential compute overhead on the vendor’s AWS or private cloud instances.
*   **The Pricing Impact:** Vendors pass these high compute bills directly to you through **strict concurrent attendee limits**, heavy fees for HD video toggles, and expensive "per-host" license models.

#### 2. Selective Forwarding Units (SFU)
Instead of mixing streams centrally, an SFU acts as an intelligent traffic router. Presenters send their video streams once to the server, and the SFU forwards those individual packets directly to participants based on downstream bandwidth.

*   **The Technical Reality:** SFUs require minimal server-side compute because they do not decode or re-encode video frames. They only consume raw bandwidth (egress).
*   **The Pricing Impact:** Vendors running modern SFU architectures can scale attendee counts without linear infrastructure costs, allowing for usage-based or high-capacity flat-rate models.

### The "Translation Tax": Where Enterprise Pricing Breaks

Expanding a webinar to global audiences has historically been cost-prohibitive due to how translation pipelines are built.

Legacy platforms (like Zoom Events, ON24, or Webex) handle real-time closed captions and audio translation via **bolted-on third-party microservices**. Their pipeline looks like this:

1. Audio stream captured via WebRTC.
2. Stream sent to an external Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) service (e.g., AWS Transcribe, Google Cloud Speech-to-Text).
3. Raw text sent to a machine translation API (e.g., DeepL, Azure Translator).
4. Translated text parsed back into the video feed as closed captions or fed into a text-to-speech engine for synthesized audio.

Because these platforms pay cloud providers for every minute of audio processed per language, they mark up the cost and bill it as an expensive add-on. Enabling multi-language live captions on enterprise tiers routinely runs between **$0.15 to $0.40 per minute per language**, turning an hour-long global all-hands or multi-regional sales demo into a multi-thousand-dollar event.

### Are open-source or DIY webinar platforms actually cheaper?

Rarely. While open-source WebRTC frameworks (like Jitsi or BigBlueButton) eliminate software subscription fees, you assume total responsibility for global server orchestration, low-latency CDN routing, recording pipelines, and custom security compliance (SOC 2, GDPR). Engineering overhead and cloud egress bills almost always eclipse the cost of a modern, low-cost commercial platform like Ollasync.

### How do I forecast attendee tiers if our event sizes fluctuate wildly?

Run a blended capacity model. Contract a base capacity that covers your median monthly webinar size (e.g., 500 attendees) rather than purchasing an expensive tier scaled to your single largest annual user conference (e.g., 5,000 attendees). For your flagship event, either purchase a single-month event pass or switch to an infrastructure provider that bills burst overages on a transparent, pay-per-attendee basis rather than forcing a permanent tier upgrade.

### How does attendee geography impact infrastructure pricing?

If you stream to viewers in regions with distributed internet infrastructure (such as APAC or LATAM), legacy CDNs charge premium rates for data egress. Furthermore, running multi-region broadcasts without localized subtitles reduces lead engagement. Using a globally distributed streaming engine with integrated multilingual processing ensures your delivery costs remain flat regardless of where your attendees tune in.
