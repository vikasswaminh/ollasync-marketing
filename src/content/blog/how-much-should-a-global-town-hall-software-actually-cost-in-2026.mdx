### 1. The Distribution Layer: Bandwidth, eCDN, and Last-Mile Network Economics

The primary infrastructure cost of any global live broadcast is network egress and edge delivery. A standard 1080p60 video stream consumes approximately 4.5 to 6 Mbps per viewer. When 20,000 employees join simultaneously, an unoptimized stream generates an aggregate load of over 90 Gbps—enough to crash corporate VPNs and overwhelm office wide-area networks (WANs).

To prevent network failure, modern platforms rely on two delivery models, each with distinct cost structures:

#### A. Enterprise Content Delivery Networks (eCDN) & P2P Mesh
* **Mechanism:** Rather than each employee pulling an individual stream from the cloud (unicast), an on-premises agent or WebRTC-based browser mesh shares video chunks locally across the office subnet.
* **Cost Impact:** Pure-play software solutions include basic WebRTC mesh routing in base platform pricing, but hardware- or agent-based setups (such as integrated Hive or Kollective nodes) carry an additional **$1.50 to $4.00 per user/year**.
* **Savings Offset:** Reduces external internet bandwidth consumption by 85% to 95%, offsetting corporate ISP overage charges.

#### B. Ultra-Low Latency Ingestion & Edge Delivery (WebRTC vs. LL-HLS)
* **Standard LL-HLS (Low-Latency HLS):** Delivers video with a 2–5 second glass-to-glass delay. It is cost-effective to scale via public CDNs (Cloudflare, Fastly, CloudFront), keeping downstream delivery costs near **$0.02 to $0.05 per viewer-hour**.
* **Ultra-Low Latency WebRTC (<500ms):** Required for natural, bi-directional Q&A between remote executives and global employees. WebRTC requires stateful edge servers and real-time media routing, increasing the technical delivery cost to **$0.08 to $0.15 per viewer-hour**.
