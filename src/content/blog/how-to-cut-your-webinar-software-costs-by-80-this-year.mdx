## The Hidden Cost Multipliers in Legacy Platforms

Legacy webinar platforms were architected over a decade ago. Their cost structures are tied to inefficient infrastructure models that penalize user growth through three primary technical bottlenecks:

### 1. Monolithic Ingestion and Bandwidth Markup
Legacy stacks use proprietary data centers and centralized media servers. When an attendee joins, the platform routes a raw, high-bitrate video stream through centralized nodes before re-encoding it for the viewer. This creates massive computing and egress overhead. Instead of absorbing these infrastructure costs, legacy vendors mark up bandwidth prices by up to 400% through arbitrary "attendee tiers." If your event grows from 500 to 1,001 attendees, your bill doubles—even though the incremental bandwidth cost to the vendor is negligible.

### 2. The Multi-Language Latency Tax
Global organizations rarely host single-language webinars anymore. Reaching EMEA, APAC, and LATAM requires localized audio channels. Legacy platforms handle localization through external audio injection: you must hire human simultaneous interpreters (averaging $150–$250/hour per language) and license expensive third-party interpretation add-ons (like Interprefy or Kudo). 

The infrastructure cost of routing multiple third-party RTMP audio feeds into a legacy room introduces synchronization drift, audio drops, and catastrophic platform fees. Running a quarterly webinar in five languages on a legacy stack typically tacks on $12,000 to $20,000 annually in interpretation logistics alone.

### 3. Seat-Based Host Penalties
Enterprise vendors charge per "named host" or "admin seat." If your product marketing, demand gen, customer success, and HR teams all run external sessions, you must purchase individual licenses for each organizer. This synthetic friction exists solely to maximize contract value (ACV) for the vendor, not because multiple organizers cost the platform extra computing power.
