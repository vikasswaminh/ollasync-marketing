## The Shift Away from the "Per-Host Seat Tax"

In enterprise software procurement, the traditional webinar platform billing architecture relies on a **Named Host License model**. Under this model, if a B2B marketing, sales, and customer success organization needs 25 different employees to host product demonstrations, training sessions, or thought leadership panels, procurement must purchase 25 discrete licenses. 

This creates three critical operational bottlenecks:

1. **Credential Sharing & Security Risks:** Teams routinely bypass seat fees by sharing master administrative logins across departments, creating significant cybersecurity vulnerabilities, compromising Single Sign-On (SSO) governance, and invalidating audit trails.
2. **Scheduling Bottlenecks:** Centralized marketing operations (RevOps/MOps) become administrative gatekeepers. Sessions must be scheduled around the availability of licensed host accounts rather than customer demand.
3. **Artificial Cost Scaling:** The organization's software spend increases as a direct function of internal headcount growth rather than external audience reach or business revenue generated.

Platforms providing **flat-rate pricing webinar hosts** solve this by shifting the value metric from **internal inputs** (how many employees run the software) to **external outputs** (how many attendees consume the content).

```
TRADITIONAL "PER-HOST" MODEL:
[Internal Employees (Hosts)] × [Seat License Fee] = Exploding Total Cost of Ownership
(Restricts team adoption; penalizes company growth)

FLAT-RATE / VALUE-BASED MODEL:
[Unlimited Internal Hosts] + [Fixed Infrastructure / Concurrency Tier] = Predictable Cost
(Maximizes internal adoption; aligns cost with audience reach)
```

## The Infrastructure Shift: How WebRTC and SFUs Enabled Unlimited Seats

To understand why platforms can now sustainably offer flat-rate pricing with unlimited team seats, we must look at modern cloud video architecture.

Legacy systems relied heavily on Multipoint Control Units (MCUs). MCUs transcode and stitch together every presenter’s audio and video stream on the server side into a single composite stream before sending it down to the attendee. This process demands immense, centralized server-side compute power per active host, rendering unlimited host provisioning economically unviable for software providers.

```
LEGACY MCU ARCHITECTURE (High Compute / Per-Host Cost)
[Host 1] ---\
[Host 2] ----> [ Central Server Transcoding & Stitching ] ---> [ Attendee ]
[Host 3] ---/  (Heavy CPU Load = Vendor charges per Host)

MODERN SFU ARCHITECTURE (Optimized Routing / Flat Platform Cost)
[Host 1] ---\
[Host 2] ----> [ Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU) Router ] ---> [ Attendee ]
[Host 3] ---/  (Zero Re-encoding = Cost shifts to Bandwidth)
```

Modern platforms leverage **Selective Forwarding Units (SFUs)** combined with client-side WebRTC pipelines and edge-optimized Content Delivery Networks (CDNs):

1. **Selective Forwarding, Not Transcoding:** SFUs do not decode or re-render video streams on the server. Instead, they act as intelligent routers, forwarding the incoming video packets from presenters directly to participants.
2. **Client-Side Composition:** The processing work of arranging tiles, side-by-side slides, and overlay graphics is handled by the browser or native client rather than server CPUs.
3. **Decoupled Control Planes:** Platforms separate the **control plane** (user management, analytics, integrations, scheduling) from the **media plane** (WebRTC video routing and HLS distribution).

Because adding an inactive or occasional host account to a control plane costs the SaaS provider fractions of a cent in database storage, platforms built on modern SFU pipelines can offer unlimited host seats at a flat rate, monetizing strictly on media egress (attendees and recorded storage).

## Operational Gotchas: What to Inspect in Flat-Rate Contracts

When transitioning to a flat-rate pricing webinar hosts architecture, enterprise buyers must audit the fine print to avoid hidden bottlenecks.

### Concurrent Live Rooms vs. Unlimited Seats
Unlimited hosts does not automatically mean unlimited simultaneous broadcasts. A platform may allow you to register 500 team members as hosts, but restrict your account to **one active room at a time**. If Team A hosts a customer onboarding session at 10:00 AM, Team B cannot host an investor briefing at the same time without purchasing an additional "Concurrent Room" add-on.

### Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Audit Logging
Platforms offering free seats sometimes restrict enterprise-grade governance to higher tiers. Verify that the flat-rate host model includes:
* Granular permissioning (Admin vs. Moderator vs. Presenter vs. Viewer).
* Workspace partitioning (preventing Sales from modifying Marketing’s templates).
* SAML-based SSO and SCIM provisioning for automated onboarding and offboarding.

### On-Stage Presenter Limits (Media Plane Caps)
While you may have unlimited host accounts registered on the control plane, the media plane always maintains a physical limit on how many presenters can turn on cameras simultaneously. Most modern WebRTC implementations max out between **6 to 12 concurrent on-stage video feeds** before browser performance degrades.
