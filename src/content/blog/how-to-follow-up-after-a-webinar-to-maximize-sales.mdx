## Chapter 1: The 48-Hour Graveyard

Here is how most B2B webinar funnels die. 

You spend three weeks and $8,000 running paid campaigns on LinkedIn. Your product marketers draft a 42-slide deck. Your sales team pesters their target accounts to register. The registration page converts at 34%, pulling in 1,200 sign-ups. 

On event day, 480 people show up. The chat is active. The speaker nails the product demo at minute 28. Someone in the comments asks: *"Does this integrate with Salesforce?"* Another attendee drops their LinkedIn profile directly into the public chat. 

At minute 55, the host thanks everyone for their time, promises that the recording will be sent out "shortly," and clicks **End Meeting**.

Then, the silence hits.

Operations needs 48 hours to clean the CSV file. Marketing takes another 24 hours to upload the raw video file to Wistia, design an email template, and configure the automated workflow. By the time your automated email lands in an attendee’s inbox on Friday afternoon, four days have passed. 

The subject line reads: *Thanks for attending! Here's the recording.*

The open rate is 18%. The click-through rate is 1.4%. Your sales team gets handed a list of 480 names, dials five of them, hears *"Remind me what company this is again?"*, and promptly abandons the lead list to go back to cold outbound. 

The webinar didn't fail. The content didn’t fail. Your follow-up killed the pipeline.

```
[Webinar Ends] ──(0-4 Hours)──> High Intent / Memory Active
       │
       └──(24 Hours)──> 50% Intent Decay
              │
              └──(48+ Hours)──> Cold Lead / Lead Amnesia
```

Most growth teams treat post-webinar outreach as an administrative chore—a box to check once the "real work" of hosting the event is over. They assume that if someone sat through 45 minutes of a presentation, that prospect will remember the value proposition next Tuesday. 

They won’t. In modern B2B SaaS, your buyer sits through three product demos, scans ten vendor websites, and deletes twenty cold pitches every single week. When you wait days to send a generic link to a 60-minute video file, you are asking your prospect to do your work for you. You are expecting them to remember why they cared, fast-forward through your housekeeping slides, find the five minutes relevant to their use case, and self-select into a demo request.

They will not do it.

Mastering **how to follow up** after a digital event is not about writing a clever email template. It is about speed-to-context: capturing high-intent signals while they exist, segmenting audiences based on actual engagement rather than binary attendance, and personalizing the next step before memory decays. 

If you want your webinars to generate revenue instead of vanity metrics, the presentation is simply the top of the funnel. The follow-up is the close.

## Chapter 6: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How soon should sales reps reach out after a webinar ends?

For high-intent prospects, sales outreach should occur within **two hours**, and never later than the end of the business day. 

If an attendee stayed for the entire session, downloaded an in-webinar handout, or participated in the Q&A, they are actively considering your solution. Reaching out while your insights are top-of-mind creates an organic bridge to a discovery conversation. For passive attendees, automated follow-up can deploy within 2 to 4 hours, followed by manual rep touches 24 to 48 hours later.

### How to follow up with no-shows without sounding generic?

When determining how to follow up with registrants who failed to attend, avoid passive-aggressive or standard automated copy like *"We noticed you couldn't make it."*

Instead:
1. **Remove friction entirely:** Do not force them to fill out another form to see the recording. Provide direct, one-click access to the replay.
2. **Lead with executive value:** Deliver the core takeaway right inside the email body. Summarize the single most actionable slide, data point, or framework from the event.
3. **Offer an alternative medium:** Some buyers skip webinars because they dislike 45-minute video presentations. Offer a translated transcript, a five-minute read recap, or an interactive slide deck.
