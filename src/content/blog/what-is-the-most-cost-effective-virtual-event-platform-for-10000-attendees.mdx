## Key Drivers of 10,000-Attendee Platform Costs

To secure the most cost-effective virtual event platform, enterprise finance and event marketing leaders must evaluate four core pricing mechanisms:

### 1. Registrations vs. Concurrent Peak Viewers
A major pricing pitfall at scale is the difference between *registered users* and *concurrent live participants*. 
* Across standard B2B benchmarks, a free 10,000-attendee virtual event typically sees a **35% to 45% live attendance rate** (3,500–4,500 peak concurrent users).
* A paid-ticket event typically reaches a **70% to 85% attendance rate** (7,000–8,500 peak concurrent users).
* **Cost Impact:** Platforms billing on *cumulative registrations* (e.g., standard HubSpot/Hubilo structures) will penalize you for no-shows. Platforms billing on *concurrent peak users* or *single-event broad capacity licenses* (e.g., Zoom Events tiers) eliminate this wasted spend.

### 2. High-Definition Bandwidth and RTMP Egress
Streaming high-definition video (1080p, 60fps) to 10,000 endpoints simultaneously consumes massive data. A single 4-hour keynoted session streamed to 10,000 concurrent viewers consumes roughly **27,000 Gigabytes (27 TB)** of egress data.
* Turnkey SaaS platforms like **Airmeet** and **Zoom Events** absorb video streaming costs directly into their license fees.
* Other platforms pass video consumption surcharges or third-party streaming fees directly to the client if usage exceeds predetermined streaming thresholds.

### 3. Feature Unbundling & Monetization Penalties
Many enterprise vendors advertise lower base costs but place essential large-scale event capabilities behind premium paywalls:
* **SSO (Single Sign-On) & Custom SAML Integration:** Frequently walled behind enterprise-tier upgrades costing an additional $5,000 to $10,000.
* **Custom RTMP Input/Output:** Required for professional studio streaming (e.g., via OBS, vMix, or Wirecast), sometimes billed as an extra add-on.
* **Ticketing & Payment Processing Surcharges:** Many platforms assess a 1.5% to 5.0% platform fee on top of Stripe/merchant fees for paid 10,000-ticket events.

## 1. The Physics of 10,000 Concurrent Connections: CCU vs. Registrations

The fundamental mistake in event budgeting is conflating *total registrations* with *Peak Concurrent Users (PCU)* or *Concurrent Content Users (CCU)*. 

```
Total Registrations (e.g., 14,000) 
  ↳ Expected Show-Up Rate: 60–70% 
  ↳ Total Attendees: 8,400–10,000
  ↳ Peak Concurrency (Keynote): 7,500–9,000 CCU
```

Platform pricing models typically fall into two categories:

1. **Per-Seat / Per-Registration SaaS Licensing:** Fixed upfront cost based on named users or registered emails.
2. **Usage-Based Infrastructure Pricing:** Variable costs tied directly to consumed gigabytes (GB), egress hours, and transcoding minutes.

If an enterprise procures a platform charging $2 to $5 per registration for 10,000 users, the baseline spend starts at $20,000 to $50,000 before add-ons. Conversely, a platform engineered around ephemeral infrastructure calculates cost purely on bandwidth delivery and pipeline orchestration. 

Selecting a **cost-effective virtual event platform** requires matching the pricing tier to real-world traffic curves: paying for a high baseline registration capacity is inefficient when attendance concentrates into a two-hour plenary session.

## 2. Streaming Protocol Economics: WebRTC vs. Low-Latency HLS/CMAF

The technical protocol chosen to distribute video to 10,000 people directly dictates the unit economics of the broadcast.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Protocol Economics at 10k Scale                                                   |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| Protocol          | Latency            | Edge Cost / User   | Best Use Case       |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
| Standard WebRTC   | Sub-500ms          | High ($$$)         | VIP / Breakouts (&lt;50)|
| Ultra-Low Latency | ~1–3 seconds       | Medium-High ($$)   | Live Q&A / Polling  |
| LL-HLS / CMAF     | ~2–5 seconds       | Optimized ($)      | Scaled Keynotes     |
| Standard HLS/DASH | 10–30 seconds      | Lowest ($)         | One-Way Broadcasts  |
+-------------------+--------------------+--------------------+---------------------+
```

* **Full Mesh / SFU WebRTC (Sub-500ms):** Highly interactive but computationally expensive. Maintaining stateful, bi-directional peer connections to 10,000 endpoints simultaneously drives compute and egress costs exponentially.
* **Low-Latency HLS (LL-HLS) and CMAF (2–5 seconds):** Utilizes chunked transfer encoding distributed over standard HTTP Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) like Cloudflare, Fastly, or AWS CloudFront. 

**The 2026 Standard:** Platforms engineered for cost efficiency apply a *hybrid distribution model*. Speakers and VIP panelists interact inside a WebRTC stage environment (zero latency), while the output is instantly ingested, transcoded, and converted to an LL-HLS stream pushed via global Anycast CDNs to the remaining 9,990 attendees. This drops the delivery cost per attendee-hour from ~$0.15–$0.30 down to ~$0.01–$0.03.

$$\text&#123;Egress (GB)&#125; = \frac&#123;\text&#123;Bitrate (kbps)&#125; \times \text&#123;Duration (s)&#125; \times \text&#123;Attendees&#125;&#125;&#123;8 \times 10^6&#125;$$

A platform that implements aggressive background tab throttling and modern codecs (AV1 or HEVC vs. legacy H.264) reduces aggregate egress consumption across 10,000 endpoints by up to 40%.

At 10,000 attendees, platform failure carries catastrophic brand risk. Turnkey platforms often charge a mandatory $3,000–$7,000 "Dedicated Production / Premium Support" package to guarantee dedicated infrastructure provisioning, pre-warmed CDN caches, and direct access to site reliability engineering (SRE) war rooms during the live broadcast.
