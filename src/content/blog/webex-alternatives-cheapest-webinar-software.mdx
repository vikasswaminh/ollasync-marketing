## Chapter 2: The Hidden Costs

A platform can look affordable during procurement and expensive after six months of real use. The difference comes from how the software fits the full event lifecycle.

### 1. Multiple products for one webinar

A webinar is not just a live stream. It is a sequence of connected tasks:

1. Create the event
2. Build the registration experience
3. Promote it
4. Send confirmations and reminders
5. Prepare speakers
6. Run the live session
7. Record and process the content
8. Analyze engagement
9. Route leads for follow-up
10. Publish and reuse the replay

When one platform handles only the live session, teams must fill the gaps with other tools. Marketing may use a form builder. Demand generation may use an email platform. Operations may rely on spreadsheets. Sales may receive a manually exported attendee list. A translation agency may handle localized captions or audio.

Each handoff introduces a cost. Someone has to configure it, monitor it, troubleshoot it, and explain it to the next person. If a field fails to sync or a reminder is sent with the wrong time zone, the problem becomes an event-day risk.

The hidden cost is not only the software bill. It is the coordination required to make separate products behave like one system.

## Chapter 5: Migration Playbook: Moving Off Webex

Replacing Webex is not primarily a software-switching exercise. It is a workflow migration. Your team must preserve registration, reminders, speaker coordination, recordings, analytics, compliance, and attendee experience while moving to a platform with a better cost structure.

The safest approach is to treat the migration as a controlled rollout rather than a single cutover.

### 5.1 Define the business case before choosing a platform

Start by documenting why you are leaving Webex. Common reasons include:

- Rising per-host or per-seat costs
- Separate charges for webinar capacity, events, or translation
- Limited global reach
- A poor attendee experience for international audiences
- Too many disconnected tools for registration, promotion, delivery, and follow-up
- Slow administrative workflows
- Unused features that still increase the total contract cost

Calculate your current annual cost using actual usage, not just the subscription price. Include add-ons, premium support, transcription, interpretation, recording storage, event production tools, and the internal time required to administer the platform.

Then compare that total with the cost of the alternatives you are evaluating. A lower headline price is not enough. The replacement must reduce total operating cost without creating hidden work for marketing, sales, events, IT, or customer success.

For global organizations, language support deserves its own line item. Traditional interpretation often requires interpreters, audio channels, scheduling, and production coordination. AI translation can reduce that complexity, but you should test quality with your actual terminology before committing.

### 5.2 Inventory your existing Webex workflows

Before exporting data or scheduling a pilot, list every way your organization uses Webex. Do not limit the inventory to recurring webinars.

Review:

- Recurring marketing webinars
- Customer education sessions
- Product launches
- Internal town halls
- Partner events
- Executive briefings
- Training programs
- On-demand recordings
- Registration pages and forms
- Email reminders and calendar invitations
- Speaker rehearsals
- Q&A and polling workflows
- Attendance and engagement reports
- CRM and marketing automation integrations
- Captioning, transcription, and translation requirements
- Data retention and access policies

Assign an owner to each workflow. Marketing may own registration, while events owns production and IT owns identity or integrations. A migration fails when no one owns the details between departments.

Create a simple requirements matrix with three columns:

| Requirement | Current Webex process | Replacement requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Webex form connected to campaign | Custom form or native registration with CRM sync |
| Reminders | Automated email sequence | Automated reminders with calendar support |
| Global access | Separate interpretation process | Native multilingual AI translation |
| Recording | Stored in Webex library | Exportable, searchable, permission-controlled library |
| Reporting | Attendance and engagement reports | Downloadable reports and integration access |

This exposes gaps before they affect a live event.

### 5.3 Choose the first webinar carefully

Your first migration should be representative, but not business-critical. Avoid using the platform for your largest annual customer event. Choose a webinar with:

- A known audience
- A manageable speaker group
- A clear success metric
- Enough lead time for testing
- No unusual compliance or production requirements

A standard product demo or educational session is often a good pilot. It lets you test registration, reminders, presenter controls, recording, Q&A, analytics, and follow-up without putting a flagship event at risk.

For global teams, include attendees from at least two regions and test the languages you expect to use. ### 5.4 Rebuild, do not blindly copy, your registration experience

Webex forms often become embedded in campaign processes over time. Recreate the registration journey intentionally.

Review:

- Required fields
- Consent language
- Privacy notices
- Hidden campaign fields
- Confirmation-page messaging
- Calendar files
- Reminder timing
- Confirmation and cancellation emails
- Routing rules for sales follow-up
- UTM and campaign attribution

Remove fields that no longer serve a purpose. Every unnecessary field can reduce conversion. At the same time, preserve fields required for routing, segmentation, compliance, or event personalization.

Test the complete journey as an attendee. Register with a personal address, a corporate address, and a mobile device. ### 5.5 Prepare speakers and producers for the new controls

Even experienced Webex presenters need a short orientation on a replacement platform. Focus training on the controls that affect live delivery:

- Joining the green room or backstage area
- Sharing slides and video
- Assigning presenter roles
- Managing microphones and cameras
- Monitoring chat and Q&A
- Launching polls
- Handling translated audio or captions
- Starting and stopping the recording
- Recovering from a dropped connection
- Handing control to another speaker

Run a rehearsal with the actual deck, videos, links, polls, and speakers. Do not rely on a generic demo. Most live issues involve the content or the handoff between people, not the platform itself.

Create a one-page event runbook that includes technical contacts, speaker order, timing cues, backup presenters, escalation steps, and the attendee support link.

You rarely need to move every Webex asset. Start with recordings that still generate views, leads, customer value, or search traffic.

Prioritize:

1. High-performing evergreen content
2. Recent sessions with active campaigns
3. Recordings required for compliance or customer access
4. Sessions with valuable captions or transcripts

That depends on the replacement platform and its browser support. Before migrating, test the attendee experience on current versions of Chrome, Edge, Safari, and mobile browsers. Confirm whether joining requires an account, an app, a plug-in, or a download.
