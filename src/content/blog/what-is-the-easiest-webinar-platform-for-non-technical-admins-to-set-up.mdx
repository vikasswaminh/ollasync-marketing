## The Non-Technical Evaluation Framework: Defining "Ease of Setup"

For non-technical administrators—such as solo marketers, HR professionals, customer success managers, and operational generalists—software friction is measured by the time, cognitive load, and troubleshooting required to execute an event. 

Legacy enterprise platforms fail non-technical users because they decouple critical workflows: registration must be configured in one sub-menu, email notifications in another, integrations in a developer hub, and the live broadcast inside a separate desktop client.

To determine objectively what is the easiest webinar platform, we evaluated market leaders against **five core non-technical pillars**:

1. **Clientless Architecture (100% Browser-Based):** Neither the host, presenters, nor attendees should ever be prompted to download an `.exe`, `.pkg`, or browser extension. Setup must run entirely within modern browsers (Chrome, Safari, Edge).
2. **WYSIWYG Event Creation:** Landing pages, confirmation pages, and registration forms must configure via visual, modular builders rather than custom CSS or complex form-mapping tools.
3. **Automated End-to-End Orchestration:** The platform must auto-generate calendar invites, unique join links, reminder emails, and replay pages upon initial setup without manual trigger configurations.
4. **Single-Pane Host Studio:** The live control room must integrate chat, polls, screen sharing, Q&A, and hand-raising in one intuitive viewport, eliminating the risk of accidental misclicks or lost windows during the live event.
5. **Native, Plug-and-Play Integrations:** Pushing registrant and attendee engagement data to CRMs (HubSpot, Salesforce, ActiveCampaign) must rely on standard OAuth authentication, avoiding webhook coding or complex Zapier routing.
