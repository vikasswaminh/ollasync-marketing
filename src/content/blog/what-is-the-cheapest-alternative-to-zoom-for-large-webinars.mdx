## 1. The Real Cost Driver: WebRTC Mesh vs. LL-HLS Pipelines

Zoom’s high price tag for large events stems directly from its architectural design. Zoom treats every webinar participant as an endpoint on an interactive Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU) network. Even when 99% of your attendees keep their cameras and microphones turned off, the Zoom infrastructure reserves bidirectional, ultra-low-latency (&lt;200ms) pipelines for those sessions.

Maintaining real-time bidirectional infrastructure for thousands of simultaneous streams requires massive server allocations, leading to Zoom’s steep tier jumps (often scaling from $340/month for 500 attendees to well over $1,500/month for 5,000 attendees on Zoom Webinars/Events).

Modern alternatives achieve radically lower price points by decoupling the **presenter stage** from the **audience delivery layer**:

```
Presenter Tier (Interactive)         Distribution Tier (Passive Scale)
+-----------------------+            +----------------------------------+
| Presenters (1-10)     | --RTMP-->  | Transcoding & CDN Edge Packaging |
| WebRTC (&lt;200ms)       |            | (LL-HLS / WebRTC via CDN Edge)   |
+-----------------------+            +----------------------------------+
                                                      |
                                     +----------------------------------+
                                     | 1,000 - 50,000+ Attendees        |
                                     | Latency: 1.5s - 3s (Sub-second)  |
                                     +----------------------------------+
```

### The Hybrid WebRTC-to-LL-HLS Advantage
Budget-focused platforms route hosts and active panelists into a private WebRTC green room for real-time interaction. That combined feed is then transcoded instantly into Low-Latency HTTP Live Streaming (LL-HLS) or distributed via WebSockets/edge CDNs.

* **Infrastructure Impact:** LL-HLS allows vendors to utilize commodity global CDNs (Cloudflare, Fastly, AWS CloudFront) instead of proprietary SFU compute clusters.
* **Cost Impact:** Delivering an hour of video over standard CDN edge nodes costs fractions of a cent per gigabyte, allowing platforms like **WebinarGeek**, **Zoho Meeting**, and **Restream** to deliver identical video quality at up to **70% less operational cost** than Zoom.
* **The Operational Trade-off:** The audience experiences a negligible 1.5-to-3-second broadcast delay. For 98% of corporate webinars, product demos, and town halls, this latency delta has zero negative impact on audience engagement, chat, or poll responsiveness.

## 4. Technical and Operational Friction Points

Transitioning away from Zoom to save money introduces operational variables that IT departments must proactively manage:

1. **Firewall & Corporate Proxy Ingress:** Zoom's installed desktop client features aggressive UDP hole punching and fallback proxies that penetrate restrictive enterprise corporate networks easily. Browser-first low-cost alternatives rely entirely on WebSocket and standard HTTPS/TLS (TCP 443) configurations. While zero-install browser solutions offer higher attendee conversion, enterprises broadcasting strictly to government or financial clients must test WebSockets reachability in advance.

Lower-cost solutions often offload workflow triggers to webhooks or automation platforms (Zapier, Make), which can introduce operational maintenance overhead.
