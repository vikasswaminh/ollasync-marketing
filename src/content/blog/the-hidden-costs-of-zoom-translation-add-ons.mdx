### Anatomy of an Add-On: The 5-Hop Latency Problem

Most enterprise teams rely on one of two setups for Zoom multilingual events:
1. **The Native Caption Add-On:** Zoom’s native translated captions ($5/user/month, restricted to business/enterprise accounts, limited to text-only translation).
2. **Third-Party Interpretation Bots:** Platforms like Wordly, Kudo, or Interprefy that join the call as virtual participants to capture, translate, and re-broadcast synthetic audio or captions.

Both approaches rely on an inefficient middleware topology:

```
[Speaker Audio] 
       │ (1. WebRTC Ingest)
       ▼
[Zoom Media Server] 
       │ (2. API / Virtual Bot Audio Scraping)
       ▼
[Third-Party Translation Middleware] 
       │ (3. STT -> NMT Engine -> TTS Synthesis)
       ▼
[Zoom Media Server / Secondary App] 
       │ (4. Re-injected Audio Channel or Caption Stream)
       ▼
[End User Client] (5. Rendered Output)
```

Every hop adds processing overhead:
* **Audio Ingestion & Splitting:** Third-party bots join as artificial attendees. They consume audio streams through Zoom’s desktop client virtualization or RTMP feeds, introducing 400ms to 800ms of ingest delay.
* **Transcription (STT):** Speech-to-text models require dynamic context windows. They wait for a complete clause or pause before generating a transcript chunk. (Add 1,200ms–2,000ms).
* **Neural Machine Translation (NMT):** Text feeds into an external translation API. (Add 300ms–600ms).
* **Audio Synthesis (TTS) & Re-injection:** If generating spoken voice, the translated text runs through a text-to-speech engine and routes back into Zoom via custom audio channels. (Add 800ms–1,500ms).

**Total end-to-end delay: 3 to 5 seconds.**

At a 4-second delay, the webinar experience breaks. The speaker references a slide chart, but global attendees hear the explanation after the slide has already changed. Q&A sessions collapse into conversational collisions because attendees respond to prompts delivered on a multi-second delay.
